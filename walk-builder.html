Footlore · phase 04 · deep dive

The Walk Builder

Everything else in Footlore answers "what's near me". This is the part that answers "I've got two hours — show me a good afternoon." It's the hardest screen in the app and the only one a competitor can't copy in a weekend.

Interaction model

Time is the currency, and every edit is priced in it

The temptation is to build a playlist editor — a list you add places to. That fails the moment the walk doesn't fit the afternoon, which is always. The builder is a budget, not a basket.

You set the shape, not the stops

Input is a time budget, a start point, whether you want to eat, and a mood. The app proposes a whole walk. Picking individual places from a list is the fallback for people who want it, never the first ask.

Every change quotes its price

Swap, add, drop, reorder — each option shows what it costs in minutes before you commit. +7 min on a button is more useful than any amount of explanation afterwards.

Lock what you care about, let the rest re-solve

Pinning a stop makes it a constraint; everything unpinned rearranges around it. This is how you get a walk that's genuinely yours in three taps instead of thirty.

The meal is an anchor, not an item

A kitchen has opening hours, so lunch is a stop with a hard time window. The route is solved around it — that constraint is what makes the walk feel planned by someone rather than generated.

Re-solving must be legible

When one change reshuffles the walk, say what moved. A silent reshuffle destroys the sense that you're in control, which is the whole reason people trust the plan enough to follow it.

Directions

Going over. Every walk overruns, so the plan has to bend without nagging. The app never silently reroutes and never scolds — it states the overrun once, offers three concrete trims with their consequences in clock time, and treats "keep it all" as a legitimate answer.

Under the hood

How a walk gets solved

This is an orienteering problem — pick a subset of scored places and a route through them that fits a time budget — with one time window on the meal. It's NP-hard in general and completely tractable at the size we need, which is under thirty candidates and under seven stops.

CANDIDATES

Take the top ~30 within reach

Pull ranked places from the existing nearby query, capped by a radius derived from the budget and pace: a two-hour stroll with 45 minutes of lunch leaves roughly 34 minutes of walking, so nothing beyond about 1.2 km of route length can be reached at all. Mood filters weight the scores rather than hard-filtering, so a thin category never empties the walk.

ANCHOR

Place the meal first

Choose the restaurant on rating, price band, and whether its kitchen is open in the target window, then fix it as a stop with a hard arrival window. Everything else is solved around a route that must pass through it at roughly the right time.

INSERT

Greedily add the best value per minute

Repeatedly insert the candidate with the highest score-per-added-minute at its cheapest position in the route, until the budget is spent. Cheap, fast, and produces sensible walks — the classic greedy-insertion heuristic for this problem.

IMPROVE

Untangle with 2-opt

Run a few passes of 2-opt over the ordering to remove crossings, respecting the pinned stops and the meal window. This is the difference between a route that looks planned and one that visibly doubles back — people notice crossings even when the total time is identical.

NARRATE

Give it a title and a thread

Once the stops are fixed, Claude writes the walk's name and the one-line reason on each row, with the other stops in context so the walk reads as a theme rather than five unrelated facts. Cached against the stop set, so a re-solve that changes one stop only rewrites what moved.

Routing distances: real walking times need a routing engine — OSRM's public demo server or a self-hosted instance on the OSM extract, with straight-line distance times 1.35 as the fallback when it's unavailable. Distances get cached per pair, and a walk only needs about 400 pairs at most, so the whole solve is one round trip.

What the tests caught

The solver was rounding to the minute, and it made tangled routes look optimal

Walking times are rounded for display — nobody wants 21.2 min on a button. The first version fed those same rounded minutes to the routing cost, which made genuinely different orderings tie at 22 minutes each. A tie means 2-opt stops at whichever ordering it reached first, so the untangling test failed with a visibly crossed route whose arithmetic was perfectly correct.

Fix: the cost function measures unrounded metres, and only the display rounds. Crossings on the fixture went from two to zero. This is exactly the class of bug that ships unnoticed — the numbers are right, the map just looks stupid.

What's in the module

plan.mjsThe single place time arithmetic happens. Everything on screen — the ribbon, the minute deltas, "back at 14:47" — is read off schedule(). It is also the only thing that knows waiting is elapsed time.
twoopt.mjsConstrained 2-opt. Pinned stops keep their index; cost is walking plus waiting; refuses outright to touch a plan the user hand-ordered.
edits.mjsThe reducer the UI is written against: applyEdit(plan, edit) returns the new plan plus a diff, an offer, or a warning. One place to change if a rule turns out wrong.
solve.mjsGreedy insertion. Pins, then the meal anchor, then value-per-minute insertion, then 2-opt — in that order, every time.
fixtures.mjsReal Bristol coordinates, so the numbers in the tests are real distances rather than invented ones.

Two rules earned their place by being tested, not argued. "Drop offers the freed minutes instead of spending them" and "a hand-ordered plan is honoured even when it is worse" both sound like fussy details in a document. As assertions that fail loudly the moment someone adds a convenience auto-fill, they're the difference between a planner that feels collaborative and one that feels like it's correcting you.

Failure modes

Where this gets embarrassing

The builder makes promises about the next two hours of someone's life. These are the four ways it breaks that promise.

Thin ground

Not enough worth stopping at

Fewer than three candidates above the story floor. Say so and offer a shorter walk or a wider radius — never pad with a postbox. "There's about 40 minutes of good here, not two hours" is a genuinely useful answer.

Infeasible

The meal window doesn't fit

Kitchens shut at 14:30 and the walk can't reach one in time. Resolve before proposing: shift the meal earlier, cut a stop, or drop to a takeaway stop — and name which of the three was done.

Drift

They wander off the route

People stop for coffee, take the other street. Detect distance from the planned leg, and after a threshold offer to re-solve from where they actually are rather than steering them back.

Conditions

Weather and closing time

A two-hour outdoor loop in the rain, or a walk built at 16:20 when everything shuts at 17:00. Both are known before the solve — bias toward covered, short, and open-late instead of proposing something doomed.

Scope

What ships in phase 04, and what waits

Ships

The whole loop, foreground only

Setup, solve, proposal with the ribbon, swap and drop, pinning, the running timeline, and foreground arrival detection. Walk state persists locally so closing the tab doesn't lose the afternoon.

Waits for iOS

Background arrival and offline

Region monitoring with the screen off, audio that continues in the background, and a walk cached for the tube. These are the reasons phase 05 exists — the web build should not fake them.

Later

Shared and saved walks

Sending someone a walk you liked, or replaying one in a new city. Attractive, but it needs an account system and a permanence guarantee that phase 04 shouldn't be carrying.

Draft three. The solver and the edit rules run: node solver/test.mjs. The open question I'd argue about first: whether the meal should be an anchor by default or an optional add — anchoring makes better afternoons, but it also makes every walk revolve around a restaurant the person didn't choose.
